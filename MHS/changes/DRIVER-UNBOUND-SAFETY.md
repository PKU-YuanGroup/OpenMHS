# Preserve missing-stop semantics in hardware-driver fakes

A direct Driver with a hardware control contract and no declared stop could return an achieved safe state when served through its fake backend. The server used simulated-mode software-latch behavior even when an earlier native call remained active. Direct Driver safety status now explicitly reports unconfirmed safety for hardware profiles with resolved risk R1 or higher and effectful capabilities, writable parameters or a reset hook but neither an estop method nor a safe-state action. This inspection uses the resolved contract and does not construct the backend. The server retains its latch and returns `MHS.SAFETY.SAFE_STATE_UNREACHED`.

R0 data-only measurement operations, readonly hardware contracts, declared local simulation profiles, and existing native-stop/safe-state-action paths retain their behavior. The risk is the resolved maximum including catalogue floors; a lower declared label cannot exempt a physical-control capability. Family bridges continue to supply their own safety status. This correction does not prove a native stop implementation, reconcile a pending native call, or enable hardware admission.

Private candidate validation on 2026-11-04: 574 driver tests passed with one skipped, including seven new methods. The unchanged-source counterfactual fails four affected method groups; a separate previous-candidate control reproduces its overbroad refusal of R0 acquisition. The new risk condition follows the standard's distinction between data-only measurement and physical effect, rather than treating every non-read protocol verb as physical control. An unchanged independent UR3 delayed-effect probe, using the repaired package and a controlled peer, now returns unconfirmed safety before and after the delayed effect with no invented stop call. Independent review, actual integration and current package/Docker/export validation remain pending at this candidate checkpoint.
